Microphone

-
connecting

Off until you turn it on, because the microphone is four wires to a breakout rather than a part on the board — on a panel without one, the data pin is floating, and what it picks up is a signal rather than silence. Nothing detects that as “no microphone”, so it would sit there holding all four knobs.

With it off the meters still move and nothing else does, which is also how you tune the response without the panel reacting to every word you say. A hand on an encoder always wins, and a browser tab sending audio outranks the room.

Spectrum

Band range-

Drag across the spectrum to set which frequencies the selected band listens to. Bands may overlap and may leave gaps — two bands over the same range with different shaping is a real thing to want. The axis is logarithmic, because that is how the frequencies you care about are spaced.

Travel is how much of its knob a band uses between silence and its own loudest moment. Under a third and the band is there without doing anything — pull its Full at handle in, or raise its boost.

Why the numbers are small

The S3's PDM input runs quiet — a tone loud enough to be uncomfortable in the room peaks around 0.03 of full scale, and that is the silicon, not the wiring. The defaults already account for it: Full at sits well below 1 and the upper bands carry a boost, because a hi-hat puts a fraction of a kick's energy into band 4. Raw levels here are unscaled on purpose, so a dead microphone reads as dead instead of as a healthy-looking zero.
