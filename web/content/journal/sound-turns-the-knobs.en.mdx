When 3.9.0 went out I said the audio story would get a proper write-up of its own. This is it: everything that went into the Audio edition over the last few weeks, in one place.

There is one idea underneath. A pattern does not know where a knob value comes from. So if sound is wired to the knobs, every pattern that responds to the knobs responds to sound, without a single pattern being touched. All the Audio edition does is open several doors for sound to reach the knobs through.

## Four ways in

**The Chrome extension.** The fastest. It captures whatever a browser tab is playing, analyses it on the computer, and streams four knob values to the panel over the LAN. YouTube, SoundCloud, a DJ set, anything a tab plays. It is not on the Web Store; load `tools/patternflow-audio-extension/` from the repository in developer mode.

**The on-board microphone.** A five-dollar PDM microphone soldered to the DevKit's TX and RX pins, and the panel hears the room itself. No computer, no phone. It has to be PDM because those are the only two free pins on the board, and if you forget to tie SEL to GND the microphone looks perfectly healthy and hears nothing. That one line is the stone most people trip on.

**The phone app.** A small Android app drives the panel with whatever the phone is playing. It exists for one job, filming: play a track on the phone, record, and the panel reacts to the very sound the video captures.

**MIDI.** The panel is a network MIDI port. macOS just finds it; Windows needs rtpMIDI. CC 20–23 pin the knobs absolutely, 24–27 turn them relatively, notes 60–63 are the buttons, Program Change picks the pattern, and it all goes back out the same way. Drop a `.mid` clip exported by the Director onto a MIDI track in Live, route it to the panel, and the show plays under the DAW's transport. OSC is still there too.

## The box editor

Extension, microphone or phone, the thing that turns sound into knobs is the same editor. Four boxes sit on a live spectrum. A box's width is the frequencies it listens to, its height the loudness window it maps. Drag a box over the bass and knob 1 becomes a bass knob. Inside each box are a response curve, an output range, attack and damping. Boxes start in auto, breathing with the music; grab an edge and they are yours.

Boxes map to knobs one to one, and that is fixed on purpose. Box 2 is knob 2.

Settings live in two homes. The extension keeps its mapping in the browser; the microphone and the phone app share the config stored on the panel. One editor, two homes.

## When several sources are live

A hand wins. Take an encoder and that knob is yours; let go and it returns to the music a few seconds later. The extension's or the phone's stream beats the microphone. The microphone takes whatever is left. So you can leave the microphone on and grab a knob whenever you like.

## What changed this week

The console's Audio page grew an Audio-React switch at the top: the switch that lets the extension and the phone drive the knobs, which until now lived only on the panel's NETWORK screen. Now it is in the browser.

And the floor moved. In 3.9.1 Wi-Fi and the console server went to a core of their own, so opening a page no longer stops the render. In 3.9.2 pushing a frame to the panel went from 9.9 ms to 7.0; not one byte reaching the panel changed, only the time. The Audio edition is v0.5.2 on that floor.

## Installing

Pick Audio on [patternflow.work/editions](https://patternflow.work/editions) and it installs over Wi-Fi in one click. The microphone can come later, or never; until it is built and enabled it costs nothing. The details, wiring photo included, are in `AUDIO_GUIDE.md` in the repository.
