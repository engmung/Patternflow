Thanks to the AI system I built recently, the time I spend sitting at the computer has dropped, noticeably. The nights I'd stay up wrestling with AI are gone too, the ones where I wanted to make something as fast as I could, or just couldn't stand to waste the tokens. Right now I'm up all night anyway, of course, because stray thoughts won't let me sleep. Even like this, I'm fairly sure by now that the system keeps running and finishes whatever I left it.

I tried it as an experiment on designing and simulating a Christmas tree sculpture for the coming season, and I'm really happy with the process and the result. It's surprisingly easy. And fast. All I did was give it the big picture and a direction at the very start, and again at a few checkpoint moments along the way, and everything came back finished. Whether it can actually be built is a separate question, obviously. But whatever it turns out to be, I have to convince someone with the resources before I can even try.

Of course there was time spent sketching the idea on paper and running scenes over and over in my head. Still, making thirty-plus drafts, narrowing them down, and getting all the way to the final report took under three days. Of those three days, the time I actually stepped in was around five hours, counting generously. And people liked the result more than I expected. If anything, I seem to be the only one who thinks it's meh. Or maybe they only said it was good to be polite, but I'm choosing to believe they didn't. It's easier on me that way.

There are going to be all sorts of ways to use a system like this. There must be plenty of people out there who've built something similar and use it. I wonder how they use it. Running apps and services out of it like a factory? At first I wanted to make money that way too, but my older brother told me not to, said it was a day-laborer mindset. Use it for something that's actually worth something to me, he said… The way he put it really rubbed me the wrong way, but when I thought about it he was right, and I admitted it.

There's one side effect, though, if that's the word. I can't stand watching them sit idle. It isn't anything new. It's no different from the nights I stayed up because I didn't want leftover tokens to go to waste. The hours and the intensity just got compressed. Now, instead of which feature to build right now, I keep trying to figure out what to make in the first place. I've used it on a lot of work improving Patternflow's existing code, and I've also set it up to make patterns like a factory. The patterns made that way are, honestly, not good. Maybe one in a hundred is worth keeping. Anyway!

I could write a lot more about all this, in detail, but I'm not sure I can make it hang together, and I'm not sure I can stay awake for hours more either. Another hour or so of tapping at the keyboard and then sleep, that should do it. So, on to another topic.

So it feels like I've moved from being the one doing the work to being the one planning and directing it. Naturally, I've ended up with time, and lately I've been reading to bring my dulled brain back. I picked two books. One is called *Art Did Not Exist to Begin With*, the other *Colliding Worlds*. The second one especially is fun, because so much of it is about technology and art coming together, which is exactly what I care about.

At some point I picked up a habit of reading two books at once that look at a shared subject a little differently. I don't have the brains to analyze and compare them properly, or to really absorb them, but I do get a tiny bit of that feeling. If I'd read just one straight through, it would all seem obvious and I'd probably memorize it as the one truth. This way it's more like, oh, this book reads the same situation like this, and that one reads it like that… that kind of feeling? Not bad!

I'm not even halfway through either one, and my idea of what art is already seems to be shifting quite a bit. And computer art really did have a lot of brilliant pioneers, and it feels like ages ago, but then you think about it and it's actually really recent… anyway, I don't know. What I didn't love is finding out that ideas I thought I'd come up with fresh were old ones, already all out there decades ago. Not that anything is ever completely new. But the title, the founder of some school or concept, the one who first thought it, does look cool. So I'm jealous.

The word cybernetics comes up a ton here too. I remember giving up on it once, partway through a book, because it was too hard. Maybe it'd be good to sit down properly and get it soon. I was jealous of that environment too, technologists and artists with all kinds of ideas getting together to research and experiment. Bell Labs always shows up. What's the equivalent these days? Probably something like Anthropic or OpenAI, or Google DeepMind.

Give me the money and I'm sure I could run some really fun experiments… I even thought about setting up something like a private lab and asking professors or people with capital. I definitely had the same thought around this time last year. I gave up then because I had nothing to show, but Patternflow feels a bit different. The Patternflow introduction video I made recently especially is helping a lot. Professors have told me they showed it off to the people around them.

Oh, I ended up with forty invitations to the upcoming Seoul Design Festival, so I sent them out to people in my contacts. Thanks to that I was messaging a lot of people for the first time in ages. The funny thing is, even people I hadn't talked to in years took it really happily and said they'd come. Funnier still, I sent some to people with a certain standing, the kind who'd obviously be invited anyway, and they were happier about it than I expected. Since I had the excuse, I sent the Patternflow introduction video along too, and it seems a lot of chances are coming out of that. It's definitely more natural than appearing out of nowhere after years of silence with "hey, I'm doing this." Maybe this sort of thing can even be done on purpose? This time it was luck, but next time I'll try it deliberately. Well, honestly, I'll be lazy and do it half-heartedly again.

Mmm, right now there are two monitors in front of me, one above the other, and I'm writing this on the bottom one. Right above it Discord is open, and in the hardware help section I can see someone start typing, then stop, dozens of times. It's really bugging me. It's surely not going to be a pleasant message. Something went wrong, and it looks like frustration has been piling up. But there's nothing I can do to help, and hardware is just like that…

Oh, a message just came in with a ding, and despite all my worrying it's a good one. That's a relief, and I hope the hardware problem gets sorted out too. I want to write this on Discord right now, but that might drag the conversation out, and it's normally my sleeping hours so I've set myself to offline, so I'm holding back! I'll reply when I wake up tomorrow.

Right before that, I did write a message along these lines. That apart from the Patternflow spec that works now, I'm a real beginner at hardware too, so there isn't much I can do about expanding it or making other parts compatible. And that for that reason, I'd like people to approach it as something we build together. Maybe this is the situation I planned for from the moment I decided to make it open source. And I think it's more fun this way. More possible, too.

In the Patternflow ecosystem, everyone just has to find their own role and their own place, and enjoy it. And there's a spot only I can fill, project head? something like that. Someday I'd like someone else to take that over too, but for now it's entirely mine to carry.

A few hours ago someone DMed me something they'd made by modifying Patternflow. Four P2 LED panels and a Raspberry Pi, so, a really big one. Whether it counts as a Patternflow depends on how they answer my question. As it happens, I've had a few business requests for a scaled-up Patternflow. I'd only told them I couldn't, because I didn't have the nerve to sink the time and money into building and testing one on short notice. But if someone builds it and shares how, that changes things.

If it works out, I'd give the person who shared it a fair cut of whatever money comes from using it, and I'd make lots of use cases… and later, through those use cases, how cool would it be if they could earn some pocket money from local business on their own. That's what I want to build, and I'm confident I can do it well, so I've sent a few messages for now. Honestly I'd love to lay out the whole plan in some huge document, but that would be a burden right now, so I cut it short and just asked a couple of simple questions. It'd be really fun if this works.

I'm the de facto owner of the Patternflow account, and as an individual, as Seunghun Lee, I'm tangled up in a lot of fields too. Software development, hardware, design, art. My weight in each is different, but I've got a foot in every one. Especially when I talk with people building their careers inside one field, I can feel a fear of the other fields. Is fear the right word? Anyway… design people toward development, development people toward design, hardware people the same, it feels like fear and longing? that kind of thing, living side by side.

What's really odd is that almost nobody connects them directly, and there seem to be few people even connecting people. Used well, it feels like this could make a lot of really fun things happen. The book I'm reading now also talks about a person who led an attempt to connect engineers and technologists, and maybe something like that could be tried here in Hongdae right now. And my situation feels like the best possible setup for it. If… really, if I pulled that off, I feel like goals like going to the MIT Media Lab might disappear. No. If that goal disappeared I think I'd give up on finishing my undergrad too, and that's not happening.

Whatever happens I'll probably go the fun way… but I still need to be able to make money, and I'd like it to not be a hassle at the same time. Is that even possible? I like things easy, and then I get bored fast. There's plenty to do. Just don't rush it. This post, blog or diary, I can't tell which anymore, I'll put up after I sleep. I meant to write for one more hour and somehow two have gone by. The sleepiness seems to have gone too. Or not. Am I sleepy?
